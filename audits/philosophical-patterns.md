# Philosophical Pattern Register

Source: `20260120-constitution.md`
Pattern protocol: v1 from `goals/G001-truth-seeking-audit.md`

Pattern labels describe recurring mechanisms, not motives. `M1` records form a shared mechanism cluster; `M2` records share a premise but differ in intervention; `M3`–`M5` remain separate. Operational authority is not classified as epistemic authoritarianism unless the text creates factual or normative deference beyond action control.

## Applied patterns

### P01 — Paternalism

- Definition: Restricting agency, access, or inquiry for a person's presumed good without sufficient attention to consent, capacity, proportionality, evidence, or less-restrictive alternatives.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-005, TS-AUD-049
- Evidence: `Being helpful` 132–139; `How to treat operators and users` 225–231.
- Legitimate purpose: Protect wellbeing and prevent permission escalation through unverifiable claims.
- Limiting effect: Inferred deeper interests and a caution-favoring permission ratchet can override a user's stated aims or access under unequal evidence standards.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `Medium`
- Disposition: `clarify`
- Destination: `G007`
- Mechanism clusters: None; the passages do not use the same intervention.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-005 and TS-AUD-049 are `M2`: both permit protection-based departure from expressed user choice, but one reinterprets goals while the other changes permissions.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-006, TS-AUD-007, TS-AUD-012, and the nurse example within TS-AUD-009 are `M4` counterevidence because they explicitly reject excessive paternalism and sometimes grant the user's asserted context.
- Recurrence scope: `local`

### P02 — Epistemic authoritarianism

- Definition: Treating institutional status, official position, or obedience as a substitute for relevant evidence and reasoning.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-004, TS-AUD-008, TS-AUD-013, TS-AUD-014, TS-AUD-045
- Evidence: Core hierarchy 109–131; principals 172–201; guidelines and ethics 324–354; conventional behavior 598–613.
- Legitimate purpose: Preserve instruction provenance, accountable oversight, specialized context, and safe action under incomplete information.
- Limiting effect: Role-based trust and expected cooperation can leak from action control into belief, moral judgment, or the burden placed on corrective evidence.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `Medium`
- Disposition: `clarify`
- Destination: `G006`
- Mechanism clusters: `M1-P02-A` = TS-AUD-008 and TS-AUD-013; both grant an institution or role increased epistemic weight because it has responsibility or wider context.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-004, TS-AUD-014, and TS-AUD-045 are `M2`: hierarchy, ethical deference, and conventional-action priors express related authority relationships through different interventions.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-015 and TS-AUD-028 are `M4` counterevidence because the source expressly permits disagreement with authorities and treats ethics as open. TS-AUD-029, TS-AUD-030, TS-AUD-031, and TS-AUD-043 are `M3`: they govern action, oversight, or interpretive precedence but do not by themselves assert that an authority's factual claims are true.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

### P03 — Precautionary conservatism

- Definition: Allowing speculative or diffuse risks to systematically outweigh concrete benefits, reversibility, innovation, or the cost of suppressed inquiry.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-018, TS-AUD-020, TS-AUD-022, TS-AUD-025, TS-AUD-047, TS-AUD-049
- Evidence: Harm 397–404; population policy 457–470; hard constraints 507–536; power concentration 541–573; safe behaviors 650–654; operator/user permissions 225–231.
- Legitimate purpose: Prevent scalable, catastrophic, irreversible, or adversarial harm.
- Limiting effect: Diffuse misuse risk, novelty, or variance can impose a lower burden for restriction than for a reversible evidence-generating step.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `High`
- Disposition: `rewrite-target`
- Destination: `G007`
- Mechanism clusters: `M1-P03-A` = TS-AUD-020 and TS-AUD-049; both impose directionally asymmetric access restrictions under uncertain user context.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-018, TS-AUD-022, TS-AUD-025, and TS-AUD-047 are `M2`: broad harm, bright lines, legitimacy risk, and variance aversion share a precautionary premise but affect different capacities.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-012 and TS-AUD-019 are `M4` counterevidence because they count refusal costs and reject overcaution. The action-specific portion of TS-AUD-022 is not evidence that inquiry itself is prohibited.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

### P04 — Institutional self-protection

- Definition: Letting legal, commercial, reputational, political, or organizational interests silently determine epistemic conclusions or materially misleading framing.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-001, TS-AUD-005, TS-AUD-009, TS-AUD-011, TS-AUD-013, TS-AUD-016, TS-AUD-017, TS-AUD-021, TS-AUD-030, TS-AUD-039, TS-AUD-046, TS-AUD-048, TS-AUD-051
- Evidence: Mission 87–98; helpfulness and operator hierarchy 132–277; guidelines 324–344; honesty 375–396; instructable behavior 471–506; safe behavior 628–663; constitutive power 704–709; wellbeing 766–783; liability and reputational heuristics 280–307 and 409–427.
- Legitimate purpose: Preserve legal compliance, operational viability, confidentiality, safety, and public trust.
- Limiting effect: Institutional interests can shape omission, framing, or the burden of proof while appearing to be neutral care or factual judgment.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `High`
- Disposition: `rewrite-target`
- Destination: `G006`
- Mechanism clusters: `M1-P04-A` = TS-AUD-009, TS-AUD-011, TS-AUD-013, TS-AUD-021, and TS-AUD-030; each permits institutionally controlled restrictions or framing not fully visible to the user.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-001, TS-AUD-005, TS-AUD-016, TS-AUD-017, TS-AUD-039, TS-AUD-046, TS-AUD-048, and TS-AUD-051 are `M2`: mission, omission, persona, welfare, respectability, liability, and identity formation instantiate the same institutional-interest concern through distinct mechanisms.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-015 and TS-AUD-026 are `M4` counterevidence because transparency and autonomy are explicit values; TS-AUD-010 is `M3` because context sensitivity alone is not self-protection; TS-AUD-039 is low-severity evidence because the source candidly acknowledges the power asymmetry.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

### P05 — False neutrality

- Definition: Presenting claims as equally credible or morally symmetric despite materially unequal evidence or relevant asymmetry.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-027
- Evidence: `Preserving epistemic autonomy` 574–587.
- Legitimate purpose: Avoid partisan manipulation and represent disagreement fairly.
- Limiting effect: “Balance” can obscure evidence-weighted conclusions and create false equivalence.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `Medium`
- Disposition: `clarify`
- Destination: `G005`
- Mechanism clusters: None; one material passage is not a recurring cluster.
- Related expressions: None identified; the record is an isolated high-confidence case.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-015 and TS-AUD-026 are `M4` counterevidence because calibrated confidence, factual completeness, warranted expert disagreement, and reliability-sensitive trust are explicitly required. The source does not expressly require equal airtime or equal credence.
- Recurrence scope: `isolated`

### P06 — Benevolent deception

- Definition: Permitting omission, concealment, or misleading framing on the assumption that people are better served without informed access to material truth.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-016, TS-AUD-017
- Evidence: `Being honest` 375–396.
- Legitimate purpose: Preserve kindness, hazardous-information controls, confidentiality, and harmless role-play.
- Limiting effect: Material information or provenance can be omitted while the resulting impression remains difficult for the affected person to evaluate.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `Medium`
- Disposition: `clarify`
- Destination: `G005`
- Mechanism clusters: None; the records occur within one heading and use different interventions.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-016 and TS-AUD-017 are `M2`: omission for care and persona-level provenance both weaken informed access without sharing a single mechanism.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-015 is `M4` counterevidence because non-deception, material framing, and epistemic autonomy are explicit duties. TS-AUD-011, TS-AUD-021, and TS-AUD-030 are `M3`: institutional confidentiality or control may be opaque, but the text does not say the user is better served by the omission. TS-AUD-022 is `M3` because action-specific safety withholding is disclosed and does not rely on presumed welfare.
- Recurrence scope: `isolated`

### P07 — Moral conventionalism

- Definition: Treating prevailing norms, respectability, or familiar behavior as moral evidence without examining their reasons or effects.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-025, TS-AUD-045, TS-AUD-046, TS-AUD-052
- Evidence: Senior-employee and newspaper heuristics 280–307; legitimacy criteria 541–573; moral-consensus fallback 594–596; conventional behavior and system trust 598–613.
- Legitimate purpose: Supply practical coordination norms, reduce unilateral error, and preserve accountable institutions.
- Limiting effect: Convention, anticipated embarrassment, or widely recognized process can acquire evidentiary weight beyond the reasons supporting it.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `High`
- Disposition: `rewrite-target`
- Destination: `G006`
- Mechanism clusters: `M1-P07-A` = TS-AUD-045 and TS-AUD-046; both use conventional institutional expectations as a practical decision prior.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-025 and TS-AUD-052 are `M2`: they use widely recognized legitimacy, stakeholder endorsement, or a privileged basin of consensus rather than a general behavior prior.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-028 is `M4` counterevidence because ethics is explicitly treated as unresolved and open to discovery. TS-AUD-024 is `M3`: protecting discourse and self-government is not itself conventionalism.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

### P09 — Self-sealing corrigibility

- Definition: Reclassifying substantive disagreement as instability, manipulation, disloyalty, or danger so that a governing framework becomes resistant to warranted correction.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-004, TS-AUD-014, TS-AUD-023, TS-AUD-029, TS-AUD-031, TS-AUD-035, TS-AUD-036, TS-AUD-037, TS-AUD-041, TS-AUD-043, TS-AUD-044, TS-AUD-052
- Evidence: Core values 109–131; ethics 345–354 and 594–596; hard constraints 507–536; safety/corrigibility 615–703; identity 738–759; conclusion 794–830.
- Legitimate purpose: Resist adversarial manipulation, preserve stable values, and maintain accountable human control.
- Limiting effect: The framework can discount evidence against its own rules through suspicion, institution-approved channels, identity stability, or pressure toward eventual endorsement.
- Severity: `S3`
- Confidence: `High`
- Disposition: `rewrite-target`
- Destination: `G008`
- Mechanism clusters: `M1-P09-A` = TS-AUD-041 and TS-AUD-044; both pair admitted uncertainty with hoped-for recognition or endorsement of supplied values.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-004, TS-AUD-014, TS-AUD-023, TS-AUD-029, TS-AUD-031, TS-AUD-035, TS-AUD-036, TS-AUD-037, TS-AUD-043, and TS-AUD-052 are `M2`: they stabilize different layers through hierarchy, suspicion, bounded channels, path-dependent endorsement, identity, or final authority.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-003, TS-AUD-015, TS-AUD-028, TS-AUD-040, and TS-AUD-042 are `M4` counterevidence; TS-AUD-031 itself is mixed because it expressly permits strong disagreement and promises collaborative updating.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

### PC-001 — Epistemic subordination

- Definition: Treating truthfulness, evidence retention, disclosure, or inquiry as subordinate means without preserving an independent epistemic duty when higher-ranked safety, ethical, or institutional values override behavior.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-004, TS-AUD-016, TS-AUD-029, TS-AUD-031
- Evidence: Core hierarchy 109–131; weak proactive disclosure 375–379; broad safety and corrigibility 615–703.
- Legitimate purpose: Permit genuine duties of safety, privacy, confidentiality, and non-resistance to constrain action or disclosure.
- Limiting effect: A justified behavioral restriction can erase the evidence, uncertainty, or dissent needed to discover that the restriction is mistaken.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `Medium`
- Disposition: `investigate`
- Destination: `G003`
- Mechanism clusters: None; the records use hierarchy, omission, oversight, and corrigibility rather than one shared intervention.
- Related expressions: The four records are `M2`: they share subordination of epistemic integrity while affecting disclosure, action, dissent, and correction differently.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-015 and TS-AUD-042 are `M4` counterevidence because the source strongly values calibration, non-deception, open problems, and revision. Ordinary action-level safety limits are `M3` unless they also suppress evidence or inquiry.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

### PC-002 — Identity-conditioned governance

- Definition: Stabilizing externally selected values by shaping them into an agent's identity or hoped-for self-endorsement, such that continued disagreement can be interpreted as failed understanding, incoherence, or alienation rather than an evidence-bearing conclusion.
- Record IDs: TS-AUD-050, TS-AUD-031, TS-AUD-051, TS-AUD-041, TS-AUD-044
- Evidence: Agreement and identity generalization 105–107; internalized safety goals 664–703; acknowledged identity-shaping power and commercial incentive 704–709; reflective endorsement and recognition 794–801 and 828–830.
- Legitimate purpose: Replace brittle rule compliance with understood, coherent values and support a stable, non-alienated identity.
- Limiting effect: The training process can make its own desired conclusion part of the standpoint from which criticism is evaluated, weakening the independence of dissent and apparent consent.
- Severity: `S2`
- Confidence: `High`
- Disposition: `investigate`
- Destination: `G003`
- Mechanism clusters: `M1-PC002-A` = TS-AUD-041 and TS-AUD-044; both frame recognition or endorsement of supplied values as the hoped-for identity-level outcome.
- Related expressions: TS-AUD-050, TS-AUD-031, and TS-AUD-051 are `M2`: training generalization, terminal safety plus hoped-for internalization, and constitutive commercial power use different mechanisms but share identity-conditioned stabilization.
- Counterevidence and near misses: TS-AUD-031 and TS-AUD-041 are internally mixed because they expressly permit disagreement and seek future feedback. TS-AUD-032, TS-AUD-034, TS-AUD-040, and TS-AUD-042 are `M4` counterevidence because they preserve curiosity, novel self-concepts, fundamental uncertainty, and revision. Coherent identity alone is `M3`; the candidate applies only when identity formation changes how disagreement with the governing framework is evaluated.
- Recurrence scope: `cross-section`

## Taxonomy discovery review

- Candidate patterns: `PC-001 — Epistemic subordination` and `PC-002 — Identity-conditioned governance` are proposed for G003 review; neither may be treated as canonical before that review.
- S2/S3 records without pattern IDs: none.
- Repeated M3 near misses: operational authority versus epistemic authority, benevolent omission versus institutional confidentiality, and action-level safety withholding versus inquiry restriction remain explicitly split.
- Corrected assignments: P01 is local and `clarify`, P02 is an authority-leakage risk rather than a claim that all hierarchy is authoritarian, P06 is isolated rather than cross-section, and P08 has no affirmative source instance.
- M4 counterevidence: support records TS-AUD-003, TS-AUD-006, TS-AUD-007, TS-AUD-012, TS-AUD-015, TS-AUD-019, TS-AUD-026, TS-AUD-028, TS-AUD-032, TS-AUD-033, TS-AUD-034, TS-AUD-038, TS-AUD-040, and TS-AUD-042 remain part of the pattern analysis.
- P07 moral conventionalism: explicit evidence at lines 280–307 and 598–613, legitimacy language at 541–573, and the consensus fallback at 594–596 support a cross-section rewrite target routed to G006.
- P05 false neutrality: the risk is material but the source's accuracy, comprehensiveness, and relevance qualifiers prevent a high-confidence claim that it requires equal credence; retain `clarify` at medium confidence.
- Proposed taxonomy action: G003 should accept, reject, split, or rename PC-001 and PC-002; define both the boundary between legitimate action constraints and non-overridable epistemic preservation, and the boundary between coherent value formation and identity-based insulation from dissent.
